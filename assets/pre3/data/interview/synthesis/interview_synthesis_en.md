## 2. Objective Descriptive Summary by Dimension

### 2.1 Reported Perceptions and Use of Campus AI
The four participants reported the following regarding campus AI platforms, discovery channels, and usage:
- **P001**: Learned of multiple campus AIs in class and trialed several. Reported that test queries yielded incorrect outputs and discontinued use; cited slow speed, poor output quality, and concerns regarding conversational context continuity.
- **P002**: Learned via Learning Mall promotions and class lecture. Used Library AI to search past exam papers and theses (retrieved successfully using the historical course code CPT411 after a module rename), and e-Bridge AI to locate the Ethics consent form; does not use campus AI for daily academic study.
- **P003**: Heard about campus tools from classmates as an assignment topic involved eHall. Did not adopt them for coursework, stating they think slowly, lack reasoning chains, and often provide external web links instead of direct answers.
- **P004**: Learned of eHall AI in September before coursework was assigned. Used it once before Week 5 and not in the four weeks since, citing no current need, along with cumbersome login, slow response, and incomplete information.

### 2.2 Academic Use of External AI in Coursework
Participants reported using ChatGPT, Doubao, and DeepSeek for ADS401 tasks as follows:
- **Translation and Task Interpretation**: P004 uploads full English assignment briefs to ChatGPT to translate instructions into Chinese and explain specific deliverables.
- **Ideation and Drafting**:
  - P001 inputs unstructured ideas to generate structured outlines and slides, and predicts potential classroom pop-quiz questions;
  - P002 generates initial experimental outlines and web content, adjusting them manually;
  - P003 inputs assignment requirements to generate experimental designs directly, followed by minor manual edits;
  - P004 generates rough experimental designs and refines them across multiple prompts.
- **Concept Learning**: P002 asks Doubao about difficult concepts, using follow-up prompts for simpler explanations and valuing its speed.

### 2.3 Reported Practices for Verifying AI Outputs
Participants described how they handle and verify AI outputs:
- **P001**: Scans all generated content in under 10 minutes to verify alignment with personal intent and logic, and cuts out redundant filler; does not check course literature or textbooks.
- **P002**: Reports trusting external AI more, spending 20–30 minutes reading and polishing paper drafts, and skimming conceptual answers; reports checking campus AI more strictly by inputting its outputs into Doubao to compare, stating campus AI feels incomplete and sometimes cannot provide specific answers.
- **P003**: Views experimental design as ideation rather than factual truth; when uncertain, asks another AI (e.g., DeepSeek) the same question to compare answers within an hour; does not consult courseware, textbooks, or literature.
- **P004**: Rarely verifies outputs, stating "AI is smarter than me" and noting that paying for a subscription (tier unspecified) creates strong trust; asks another AI if questions arise.

## 3. Desired Features for an ADS401 AI Teaching Assistant

Participants described the following feature expectations for an AI teaching assistant tailored to ADS401:

1. **Bilingual Statistical Explanations (P001)**: Chinese explanations of statistical calculation principles and hypothesis testing, mapped clearly to English academic terms.
2. **Software Guidance and Review Outlines (P002)**: Step-by-step guidance on SPSS operations for fast-paced labs, alongside summaries of key concepts per lecture.
3. **Course Artifact Integration (P004)**: Unified integration of lecture outlines, statistical formulas, and ALEKS homework problems, with guidance on experimental workflows.
4. **Internal Course Resource Access (P003)**: Addressing internal university course materials that external commercial models cannot access.
