### 2. Critical Incident Technique (CIT) Walkthrough

**[Interviewer]:** Please recall a recent time when, for ADS401—such as reading literature, designing a questionnaire, or experimental design—you used AI to complete tasks. Could you describe the specific process? You can talk about external AI or internal AI.

**[Interviewee]:** For external AI, I might ask it to give me a proposal, or check if we can do some experimental design based on the university website. After it gives an answer, I might modify it based on the specific situation—because some data it produces might have discrepancies. Also, we had to build a website; I didn't know how to do that before, so I had external AI help build it. I provided specific content to it, and it helped make it.

**[Interviewer]:** What about the university's AI?

**[Interviewee]:** Mm, haven't used it much. Maybe I used e-Bridge's AI once to have it search—what was that form called again, the consent form you have to fill out before conducting an experiment.

**[Interviewer]:** Ethics (ethical review form), right right right.

**[Interviewee]:** Oh, right right right.

**[Interviewer]:** Mm, okay. Can you still recall what the first query you gave to the AI tool was back then? What help did its output provide?

**[Interviewee]:** Oh, for external AI, I'd probably say directly: "Combine this with my course requirements and help me make a proposal" or something like that, or "combine this content and build a website for me". For XJTLU AI, it might be "help me find such-and-such materials" or "where should I go look for it".

**[Interviewer]:** When you started researching ADS401 materials, did you prioritize XJTLU AI or external AI?

**[Interviewee]:** External, prioritize external first, right?

**[Interviewer]:** Right. Do you have any cases where you considered using campus AI first to look up relevant materials, and then used external AI?

**[Interviewee]:** That would be the consent form earlier; I probably prioritized the one on e-Bridge. Because on e-Bridge, searching directly is quite troublesome—if I search directly I can't find it, so I might ask the AI first where it is, and then go find it.

**[Interviewer]:** But for other scenarios, like course-related materials or reference literature, would you still prioritize external AI?

**[Interviewee]:** For reference literature, I'd probably search the library first, then ask the library AI.

**[Interviewer]:** So that also counts as an internal system AI. Mm, OK, great. Let me ask one more: why do you prioritize on-campus tools when searching for internal university documents?

**[Interviewee]:** Because for literature from the university library, official sources are more reliable. If I directly ask external AI, sometimes the paper might be fabricated by itself, lacking authenticity; or I have no way to judge its quality. So I go to the university library first.

**[Interviewer]:** Mm, okay.

### 3. Verification Behavior and Trust Mechanisms

**[Interviewer]:** For the tasks you just mentioned, before doing them, did you have alternative options in mind that helped you decide to use this AI? What considerations were involved—such as response speed, familiarity, accuracy, etc.?

**[Interviewee]:** Didn't think too much, just used it directly. For example, having AI help me understand questions: during the course if there's a concept I don't understand, I might prioritize Doubao because it might be faster. If I still don't understand after it explains, I can follow up with further questions, and it will explain in more detail or in layman's terms.

**[Interviewer]:** So its response speed and accuracy matter to you?

**[Interviewee]:** Yes, right right right.

**[Interviewer]:** Mm, okay. In those tasks you mentioned, after AI gave the answer, how specifically did you treat the content? Did you adopt it directly, rewrite and polish it, or just treat it as inspiration?

**[Interviewee]:** For example, if it's explaining concepts, if I still don't understand I'll keep following up to have it explain more clearly. For things like experimental proposals or building a website, I'll first skim over what it looks like, and make some rough edits myself, because sometimes its logic or layout isn't smooth. For webpages, it might be aesthetics or the text content—I'll give it specific instructions on which block to modify, and have it redo it.

**[Interviewer]:** So when you see the results, you still think about whether to verify it, right?

**[Interviewee]:** I'd use it directly.

**[Interviewer]:** If you do verify it, how much time do you spend, and what criteria do you compare against? Like course slides, materials, papers?

**[Interviewee]:** For papers, I might polish it a bit myself and go through it, taking maybe 20 to 30 minutes. For course concepts, I just skim through; once I roughly understand it, that's enough.

**[Interviewer]:** Mm, okay. Throughout the task, here's a hypothetical question: if one day your external AIs—like Doubao—suddenly couldn't be used, which parts of your workflow would be substantially impacted?

**[Interviewee]:** That would be pretty difficult. Because the instructor lectures quite fast in class; if there's something I don't understand, I can't ask promptly. If questions pile up, it becomes hard to handle later. Or I'll forget what I didn't understand.

**[Interviewer]:** Mm-hmm. If you're stuck and don't have AIs like Doubao, would you think of using XJTLU internal AI for study assistance?

**[Interviewee]:** I might ask it, but I feel it probably wouldn't solve it very well.

**[Interviewer]:** Not as good as external AIs? Seek help after class? So would you still consider it, or has it never entered your consideration set?

**[Interviewee]:** Roughly consider it, take a look, consider it.

**[Interviewer]:** Mm, okay. As a user who has used both types, if you had to choose: over the past four weeks, which scenario fits your state better? First, collaborating and switching between them in the same task; second, completely separated, each used on its own; third, primarily using one side, and only occasionally trying the other once or twice and then abandoning it.

**[Interviewee]:** Each on its own. The highest frequency is still external AI. Campus AI might be more focused on university official matters—like looking for who is who, or university official policies; I might consider using it then.

**[Interviewer]:** Mm-mm, okay. Under what specific conditions would you prioritize campus AI? Or would you not use it under any conditions?

**[Interviewee]:** Like what I mentioned earlier about the library, and on e-Bridge, I might use it. For coursework study, I rarely use it.

**[Interviewee]:** I think I might trust external AI a bit more, but I'll still verify, like comparing several results, because they can make mistakes too.

**[Interviewer]:** Still feed it back into Doubao. Right right right. Could you say why?

**[Interviewer]:** Mm-mm.
